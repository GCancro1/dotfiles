# Trading Playbook

Personal best practices and rules for trading decisions.

## Core Principles

### 1. Trend Is Your Friend — But Wait for Confirmation
- Don't chase green candles. Wait for a pullback to a support level or a breakout with volume confirmation.
- For long-term positions, accumulate on red days within an uptrend rather than buying the top.
- Use the 20-day and 50-day moving averages as trend guides. Price above both = bullish. Price below both = bearish.

### 2. Risk Management First
- **Position sizing rule:** Risk 1-3% of total account per trade. Calculate position size from your stop loss, not from how many shares you "want" to own.
- **Always define max loss before entering.** If you can't define it, don't take the trade.
- For speculative options plays, limit to 5-10% of portfolio max. Options go to zero — size accordingly.

### 3. Entry Timing Rules
- **Don't buy just because a stock "dropped a lot"** — falling knife. Wait for stabilization (higher low, volume spike, or support hold).
- **Don't buy calls after a big run-up** — IV is usually inflated and you're paying premium for a move that already happened.
- **Better entry signals:**
  - Pullback to a key moving average (20, 50, or 200 MA)
  - Breakout above resistance with above-average volume
  - RSI oversold (<30) at support for mean reversion plays
  - Earnings gap down on solid fundamentals (if long-term thesis intact)

### 4. Exit Rules
- **Profit taking:** Scale out at +20%, +50%, +100%. Don't let winners turn into losers.
- **Stop losses:** Set at -10% for stocks, -20-30% for options. Adjust based on volatility.
- **Time stop for options:** If the position hasn't moved in your favor within 1/3 of the time to expiry, reassess. Theta is eating you alive.

### 5. Options-Specific Rules
- **Prefer selling premium** over buying it when possible (cash-secured puts, covered calls). The house edge is on the seller's side.
- **Avoid long-dated OTM calls** as a default strategy. They have low probability of profit.
- **Before buying an option, ask:**
  - What's my breakeven?
  - What needs to happen for this to be profitable?
  - Am I paying fair IV or overpaying?
  - What's my max loss?
- **Simpler is better:** Cash-secured puts on stocks you want to own, covered calls on stocks you already own, vertical spreads for defined risk.

### 6. Portfolio Guidelines
- **Max 5-7 positions** at any time. Concentrated beats diversified for smaller accounts.
- **No single position > 25% of portfolio** (including options exposure).
- **Keep 20-30% cash** for opportunities. Always have dry powder.
- **Track your trades.** Note entry thesis, exit reason, and what you learned. Patterns emerge.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

| Mistake | Why It Hurts | Fix |
|---------|--------------|-----|
| Chasing FOMO stocks | Buy tops, sell bottoms | Wait for pullback or next setup |
| Overtrading | Fees + taxes + emotional decisions | Set max trades per week (2-3) |
| Averaging down on losers | Doubles down on bad thesis | Average down only on confirmed winners |
| Ignoring earnings dates | IV crush destroys options | Check calendar before any options trade |
| No stop loss | Small loss becomes account blowup | Always have an exit before entry |
| Trading too big | One bad trade = major damage | 1-3% risk per trade, always |

## Reminder

This playbook is a living document. Update it as you learn what works and what doesn't from your own trading results.
